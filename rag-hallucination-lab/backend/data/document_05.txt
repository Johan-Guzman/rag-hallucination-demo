Limitations of RAG in Mitigating Hallucinations

While RAG reduces hallucination frequency compared to parametric LLMs, it does not eliminate them. Key limitations include:

1. Retrieval failures: Relevant information may not be retrieved due to vocabulary mismatch, poor embeddings, or insufficient coverage.

2. Groundedness failures: Even with correct retrieval, the generator may produce unsupported claims. The generator's parametric knowledge can override retrieved context.

3. Context window limits: Only a limited number of documents can be provided as context, potentially missing relevant information.

4. Citation hallucinations: Models may invent citations that appear plausible but don't correspond to actual retrieved documents.

5. Semantic drift: Multi-hop reasoning can accumulate errors across retrieval and generation steps.

Research by Shuster et al. (2021) shows retrieval augmentation reduces but doesn't eliminate hallucinations. Magesh et al. (2025) demonstrate that "hallucination-free" RAG is not achievable with current architectures. Huang et al. (2025) survey confirms hallucination remains an open problem even with retrieval.
